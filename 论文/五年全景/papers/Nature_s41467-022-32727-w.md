              - paragraph [ref=e146]:
                - text: Noncanonical redox cofactors are attractive low-cost alternatives to nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (phosphate) (NAD(P)
                - superscript [ref=e147]: +
                - text: ) in biotransformation. However, engineering enzymes to utilize them is challenging. Here, we present a high-throughput directed evolution platform which couples cell growth to the in vivo cycling of a noncanonical cofactor, nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN
                - superscript [ref=e148]: +
                - text: ). We achieve this by engineering the life-essential glutathione reductase in Escherichia coli to exclusively rely on the reduced NMN
                - superscript [ref=e149]: +
                - text: (NMNH). Using this system, we develop a phosphite dehydrogenase (PTDH) to cycle NMN
                - superscript [ref=e150]: +
                - text: with ~147-fold improved catalytic efficiency, which translates to an industrially viable total turnover number of ~45,000 in cell-free biotransformation without requiring high cofactor concentrations. Moreover, the PTDH variants also exhibit improved activity with another structurally deviant noncanonical cofactor, 1-benzylnicotinamide (BNA
                - superscript [ref=e151]: +
                - text: ), showcasing their broad applications. Structural modeling prediction reveals a general design principle where the mutations and the smaller, noncanonical cofactors together mimic the steric interactions of the larger, natural cofactors NAD(P)
                - superscript [ref=e152]: +
                - text: .
