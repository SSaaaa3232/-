                - paragraph [ref=e94]:
                  - text: It is challenging to biosynthesize industrially important aldehydes, which are readily consumed by the numerous alcohol dehydrogenases (ADHs) in cells. In this work, we demonstrate that a nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN
                  - superscript [ref=e95]: +
                  - text: )-dependent redox cofactor cycling system enables aldehyde accumulation in
                  - emphasis [ref=e96]: Escherichia coli
                  - text: crude lysates and whole cells. By specifically delivering reducing power to a recombinant enoate reductase, but not to endogenous ADHs, we convert citral to citronellal with minimal byproduct formation (97–100% and 83% product purity in crude lysate- and whole cell-based biotransformation, respectively). We envision the system’s universal application to lowering the noise in biomanufacturing by silencing the host’s metabolic background.

                - paragraph [ref=e106]:
                  - text: "Traditionally, biotransformation has been carried out either in vivo with engineered microbial whole cells or in vitro with purified enzymes. Recently, crude lysate-based biotransformation has emerged as an alternative which brings the benefits of both worlds: it contains a cell-like metabolism which includes glycolysis and the TCA cycle that generate energy, cofactors, and key intermediates from inexpensive feedstocks;"
                  - superscript [ref=e107]:
                    - link "1" [ref=e108] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R1"
                    - text: –
                    - link "4" [ref=e109] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R4"
                  - text: it is easier to monitor, manipulate, and optimize because a cell membrane barrier does not exist and operational parameters can be varied well outside what’s tolerable by living cells;
                  - superscript [ref=e110]:
                    - link "5" [ref=e111] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R5"
                    - text: –
                    - link "7" [ref=e112] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R7"
                  - text: it also eliminates the high cost of purifying proteins for in vitro biotransformation in large scale.
                  - superscript [ref=e113]:
                    - link "1" [ref=e114] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R1"
                - paragraph [ref=e115]:
                  - text: However, just like in whole cells, crude lysate-based biotransformation faces the challenge of controlling side reactions, which are catalyzed by the numerous endogenous enzymes of the host.
                  - superscript [ref=e116]:
                    - link "1" [ref=e117] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R1"
                    - text: ","
                    - link "2" [ref=e118] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R2"
                  - text: A traditional method to tackle this challenge in whole cells involves identifying and genetically disrupting competing enzymes,
                  - superscript [ref=e119]:
                    - link "8" [ref=e120] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R8"
                    - text: ","
                    - link "9" [ref=e121] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R9"
                  - text: which, albeit effective, can often be time-consuming and labor intensive. Instead of replicating the same approach in a crude lysate-based process for eliminating competing enzymes, here we report an alternative method, which is based on an orthogonal redox cofactor system.
                  - superscript [ref=e122]:
                    - link "10" [ref=e123] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R10"
                - paragraph [ref=e124]:
                  - text: We chose citronellal as the model product, which is a terpenoid aldehyde with wide usage in the flavor and fragrance industries, as the precursor of nonracemic menthol, and as an insect repellant.
                  - superscript [ref=e125]:
                    - link "11" [ref=e126] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R11"
                    - text: ","
                    - link "12" [ref=e127] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R12"
                  - text: Using enoate reductases (ERs), citronellal can be produced from citral (
                  - link "Figure 1" [ref=e128] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#F1"
                  - text: ), a low-cost substrate that can be readily obtained in large quantities. However, since both citral and citronellal are aldehydes, which are rapidly reduced to alcohols by a myriad of broad-substrate-range alcohol dehydrogenases (ADHs) in the cell, this conversion is inefficient in microbial hosts in vivo
                  - superscript [ref=e129]:
                    - link "11" [ref=e130] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R11"
                  - text: and with crude lysates in vitro.
                  - superscript [ref=e131]:
                    - link "13" [ref=e132] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R13"
                  - text: Previously, Paul and co-workers employed a biomimetic cofactor, 1-benzyl-1,4-dihydronicotinamide, to increase the production of citronellal using poorly purified ER from
                  - emphasis [ref=e133]: E. coli
                  - text: .
                  - superscript [ref=e134]:
                    - link "13" [ref=e135] [cursor=pointer]:
                      - /url: "#R13"
                  - text: This system operated on the basis of the principle that only ER could utilize the biomimetic cofactor as reducing power, while the
                  - emphasis [ref=e136]: E. coli
                  - text: endogenous ADHs, which were also present in the crude ER-extract, could not. This method eliminated the cost of extensively purifying ER. However, because this system did not utilize a method of cofactor regeneration, a stoichiometric amount of biomimetic cofactor was required, which could add a formidable cost in large scale. In addition, application of this system in vivo was not examined.

                  - superscript [ref=e158]: +

                  - text: ) only yielded the alcohol byproducts, namely, citronellol, nerol, and geraniol. We envision this approach may be adapted for different target products and different chassis hosts with relative ease.

              - generic [ref=e367]:
                - heading "CONCLUSIONS" [level=2] [ref=e368]: CONCLUSIONS
                - paragraph [ref=e369]:
                  - text: This work demonstrates the use of an NMN
                  - superscript [ref=e370]: +
                  - text: "-dependent redox cofactor system as a facile method to develop biocatalysts for aldehyde production. As a proof-of-concept, we achieved effective conversion of citral to citronellal (both are aldehydes) by silencing the highly active ADHs in both"
                  - emphasis [ref=e371]: E. coli
                  - text: crude lysates and whole cells. Notably, we decreased the level of alcohol byproducts in crude lysate-based biotransformation from 60% to essentially zero by only optimizing the components in the NMN
                  - superscript [ref=e372]: +
                  - text: redox loop and without relying on information about the host’s metabolic background. The ease in optimization highlights the advantage of an insulated, orthogonal metabolic system, which may be particularly beneficial in engineering nonmodel organisms without well understood metabolisms. As more enzymes are engineered to efficiently utilize NMN(H), the methods established here may be readily adapted to produce various chemicals.
                - paragraph [ref=e373]:
                  - text: The orthogonal redox cofactor system is highly complementary to the commonly used host engineering methods. For example, while the orthogonal redox cofactor system bypassed the laborious process of knocking out the numerous, highly promiscuous ADHs and aldehyde reductases,
                  - superscript [ref=e374]:

                      - /url: "#R21"
                  - text: efforts to genetically eliminate the fewer, more substrate-specific, and more easily identifiable competing enzymes that consume citral and citronellal (namely, aldehyde dehydrogenases and citral lyase) may further improve biotransformation performance.
