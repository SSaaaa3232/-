              - generic [ref=e114]:
                - heading "Introduction" [level=2] [ref=e115]: Introduction
                - paragraph [ref=e116]:
                  - text: Lycopene is one of the major precursors of downstream carotenoids produced by many plants and microorganisms. It is of great economic interest due to its attractive anti-oxidative, anti-cancer, and anti-inflammatory activities, and has been widely used in the pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, cosmetics, and food industries (Nagao and Yoshikawa
                  - link "2009" [ref=e117] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR21"
                  - text: ).
                - paragraph [ref=e118]:
                  - text: Lycopene has been produced by extraction (Clinton
                  - link "1998" [ref=e119] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR6"
                  - text: ), chemical synthesis (Michael and Bausch
                  - link "2003" [ref=e120] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR20"
                  - text: ), and fermentation (Mantzouridou and Tsimidou
                  - link "2008" [ref=e121] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR18"
                  - text: ). Because the lycopene content of tomatoes is low (approx. 0.02 g/kg) and because of the presence of many other carotenoids (Clinton
                  - link "1998" [ref=e122] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR6"
                  - text: ), the extraction method is relatively expensive. Engineering microorganisms to produce lycopene to meet the increasing demand has consequently attracted much attention, with a particular focus on
                  - emphasis [ref=e123]: Escherichia coli
                  - text: (Ajikumar et al.
                  - link "2008" [ref=e124] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR2"
                  - text: ; Bhataya et al.
                  - link "2009" [ref=e125] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR4"
                  - text: ).This is not only due to the potential benefits of lycopene for human health as an antioxidant (Rao and Agarwal
                  - link "2000" [ref=e126] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR22"
                  - text: ), but more importantly because lycopene has been extensively investigated as a model target for metabolic engineering (Farmer and Liao
                  - link "2000" [ref=e127] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR9"
                  - text: ; Wang et al.
                  - link "2009" [ref=e128] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR25"
                  - text: ; Alper et al.
                  - link "2005" [ref=e129] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR3"
                  - text: ). Up to now, several strategies have been employed to improve lycopene production in
                  - emphasis [ref=e130]: E. coli
                  - text: ": first, overexpressing the native 2-C-methyl-D-erythritol-4-phosphate (MEP) pathway (Choi et al."
                  - link "2010" [ref=e131] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR5"
                  - text: ; Jin and Stephanopoulos
                  - link "2007" [ref=e132] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR13"
                  - text: ) or introducing a heterologous mevalonate (MVA) pathway into
                  - emphasis [ref=e133]: E. coli
                  - text: to increase the supply of isoprenoid synthesis precursors isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP) and dimethylallyl pyrophosphate (DMAPP) (Yoon et al.
                  - link "2007" [ref=e134] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR28"
                  - text: ; Martin et al.
                  - link "2003" [ref=e135] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR19"
                  - text: ); second, identification of gene knockouts or amplification targets for improving lycopene production through systematic and combinatorial methods (Kang et al.
                  - link "2005" [ref=e136] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR14"
                  - text: ; Jin and Stephanopoulos
                  - link "2007" [ref=e137] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR13"
                  - text: ; Choi et al.
                  - link "2010" [ref=e138] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR5"
                  - text: ; Alper et al.
                  - link "2005" [ref=e139] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR3"
                  - text: ); third, the optimization of fermentation processes, for example, by adding auxiliary carbon sources (Kim et al.
                  - link "2011" [ref=e140] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR15"
                  - text: ) as well as maintaining high O
                  - subscript [ref=e141]: "2"
                  - text: levels and appropriate pH values was used to improve lycopene production (Yoon et al.
                  - link "2006" [ref=e142] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR27"
                  - text: ). In addition, optimization of the NADPH and ATP levels is an effective approach for enhancing lycopene production (Tao et al.
                  - link "2014" [ref=e143] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR23"
                  - text: ).
                - paragraph [ref=e144]:
                  - text: Hydrophobic terpene products are considered to accumulate in the membrane compartment of
                  - emphasis [ref=e145]: E. coli
                  - text: (Ahrazem et al.
                  - link "2016" [ref=e146] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR1"
                  - text: ). In the previous work (Wu et al.
                  - link "2017" [ref=e147] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR26"
                  - text: ), we engineered the membrane by overexpressing membrane-bending proteins, which increased the β-carotene specific production. Lycopene is a representative hydrophobic molecule with a long straight chain composed entirely of carbon and hydrogen atoms. In fact, the molecular structure of lycopene is quite similar to alkanes, oils and fats, but different from that of β-carotene, which contains hydrocarbon rings.
                - paragraph [ref=e148]:
                  - text: In this work, we aimed to apply the membrane engineering strategy to a lycopene hyper-producing strain of
                  - emphasis [ref=e149]: E. coli
                  - text: by introducing membrane-bending proteins and enhancing the membrane-synthesis pathway, to study how this strategy would affect the production and accumulation of lycopene (Fig.
