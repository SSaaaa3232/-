                - heading "Abstract" [level=2] [ref=e100]: Abstract
                - paragraph [ref=e101]:
                  - emphasis [ref=e102]: Corynebacterium glutamicum
                  - text: is an important strain for the industrial production of amino acids, but the fermentation of L-methionine has not been realized. The purpose of this study is to clarify the effect of reducing power NADPH on L-methionine synthesis. Site-directed mutagenesis of
                  - emphasis [ref=e103]: zwf
                  - text: and
                  - emphasis [ref=e104]: gnd
                  - text: genes in pentose phosphate pathway relieved feedback inhibition, increased NADPH supply by 151.8%, and increased L-methionine production by 28.3%; Heterologous expression of
                  - emphasis [ref=e105]: gapC
                  - text: gene to introduce NADP
                  - superscript [ref=e106]: +
                  - text: dependent glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase increased NADPH supply by 75.0% and L-methionine production by 48.7%; Heterologous expression of
                  - emphasis [ref=e107]: pntAB
                  - text: gene to introduce membrane-integral nicotinamide nucleotide transhydrogenase increased NADPH by 89.2% and L-methionine production by 35.9%. Finally, the engineering strain YM6 with a high NADPH supply was constructed, which increased the NADPH supply by 348.2% and the L-methionine production by 64.1%. The analysis of metabolic flux showed that YM6 significantly increased the glycolytic flux, including the metabolic flux of metabolites such as glycosyldehyde-3-phosphate, dihydroxyacetate phosphate, 3-phosphoglycate and pyruvate, and the significant increase of L-methionine flux also confirmed the increase of its synthesis. This study provides a research basis for the systematic metabolic engineering construction of L-methionine high-yield engineering strains.

              - generic [ref=e112]:
                - heading "1. Introduction" [level=2] [ref=e113]: 1. Introduction
                - paragraph [ref=e114]:
                  - text: L-methionine is one of the eight essential amino acids that cannot be synthesized in human and animal bodies and is widely used in food, medicine, feed, and other industries [
                  - link "1" [ref=e115] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B1-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: ","
                  - link "2" [ref=e116] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B2-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: ","
                  - link "3" [ref=e117] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B3-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. However, L-methionine is still synthesized by chemical methods at present, and industrial microbial fermentation has not been realized ["
                  - link "4" [ref=e118] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B4-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. The chemical synthesis of methionine mainly adopts the acrolein method ["
                  - link "5" [ref=e119] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B5-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. Many harmful substances are produced in the synthesis process, and the synthetic product is DL-methionine, which needs further chiral resolution by aminoacylase ["
                  - link "6" [ref=e120] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B6-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. In addition to the direct production of L-methionine, the microbial fermentation method also avoids the use of organic compounds that are unfavorable to the environment. The large-scale fermentation production of industrial amino acids such as glutamate and lysine has matured, providing the possibility for the industrial fermentation production of L-methionine."
                - paragraph [ref=e121]:
                  - emphasis [ref=e122]: Corynebacterium glutamicum
                  - text: is the main producer of the amino acid fermentation industry in the world, such as glutamate, lysine, and threonine [
                  - link "7" [ref=e123] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B7-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. In order to realize the industrialized fermentation production of L-methionine, its production cost is required to be comparable to that of chemical synthesis, but the current yield is far from meeting the requirements. After systematic metabolic engineering, the current maximum production of L-methionine can only reach about 6 g/L ["
                  - link "8" [ref=e124] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B8-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: ","
                  - link "9" [ref=e125] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B9-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. Therefore, it is necessary to clarify the regulation mechanism of L-methionine and find new metabolic engineering targets. One of the reasons why L-methionine is more difficult to synthesize by"
                  - emphasis [ref=e126]: C. glutamicum
                  - text: compared with other amino acids is that the structure of L-methionine contains sulfur atoms, and the inorganic sulfur source needs to be reduced before it can be used for L-methionine synthesis, which requires more reducing power [
                  - link "10" [ref=e127] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B10-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. NADPH plays a major reducing role in cells. It takes 1 mol and 4 mol of NADPH to produce 1 mol of L- glutamate and L-lysine, respectively ["
                  - link "11" [ref=e128] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B11-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "], while the NADPH is required to produce 1 mol of L-methionine is 8 mol ["
                  - link "9" [ref=e129] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B9-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. Therefore, overproduction of L-methionine requires more NADPH supply."
                - paragraph [ref=e130]:
                  - text: In
                  - emphasis [ref=e131]: C. glutamicum
                  - text: ", NADPH is mainly derived from the two-step dehydrogenation reaction in the pentose phosphate pathway, that is, the step of generating gluconolactone-6-phosphate from glucose-6-phosphate and the step of generating ribulose-5-phosphate from 6-phosphogluconate ("
                  - link "Figure 1" [ref=e132] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#foods-11-01031-f001"
                  - text: ). However, too much NADPH will inhibit the above two reactions, so enzyme modification to remove the feedback inhibition is an important step to increase the intracellular NADPH content [
                  - link "12" [ref=e133] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B12-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. In the reaction of synthesizing 3-phosphoglycerate from glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase belongs to the NAD"
                  - superscript [ref=e134]: +
                  - text: "-dependent type and cannot synthesize NADPH. Heterologous expression of"
                  - emphasis [ref=e135]: gapC
                  - text: from
                  - emphasis [ref=e136]: Clostridium acetobutylicum
                  - text: can introduce NADP
                  - superscript [ref=e137]: +
                  - text: "-dependent glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase, thereby increasing NADPH supply ["
                  - link "13" [ref=e138] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B13-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. In addition, the heterologous expression of nicotinamide nucleotide transhydrogenase in"
                  - emphasis [ref=e139]: E. coli
                  - text: can also promote the conversion of NADP
                  - superscript [ref=e140]: +
                  - text: to NADPH [
                  - link "14" [ref=e141] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B14-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. Therefore, this work used the above methods to study the effect of increased NADPH supply on L-methionine synthesis in"
                  - emphasis [ref=e142]: C. glutamicum
                  - text: and studied the change of central carbon metabolism after increased NADPH supply through
                  - superscript [ref=e143]: "13"
                  - text: C metabolic flux analysis, to provide some theoretical reference for the construction of L-methionine engineering strains.

                - heading "4. Discussion" [level=2] [ref=e672]: 4. Discussion
                - paragraph [ref=e673]:
                  - text: In the past few decades, although researchers have done a lot of work in the breeding and selection of L-methionine-producing strains, they still cannot meet the needs of industrial production [

Studies have used reduced methanethiol and dimethyl disulfide as the only sulfur source and found that"
                  - emphasis [ref=e676]: C. glutamicum
                  - text: was able to grow. However, due to a limited supply of sulfur for cysteine synthesis and the toxicity of high concentration sulfide, the specific growth rate of cells decreased significantly [
                  - link "20" [ref=e677] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B20-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. Therefore, as the main source of reducing power in biosynthesis, NADPH is a key to sulfate reduction. Engineering NADPH synthesis pathways have been widely used in amino acid biosynthesis. Wang et al., constructed a high intracellular NADPH level of the"
                  - emphasis [ref=e678]: C. glutamicum
                  - text: strain, compared with the starting strain, the production of L-lysine was increased by 1.86-fold, and the production of L-leucine was increased by 1.4-fold [

                  - emphasis [ref=e681]: gnd

                  - emphasis [ref=e682]: gapC

This may be caused by heterologous expression or an imbalance of the redox state. Unbalanced NADPH may inhibit the yield of products [
                  - link "21" [ref=e683] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B21-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]."
                - paragraph [ref=e684]:
                  - text: Since NADPH is the main source of reducing power in biosynthesis, and its deficiency has become a major limiting factor in the production of amino acids such as lysine, how to increase the supply of intracellular NADPH to meet the production demand has become a focus of research [

                  - text: ","
                  - link "22" [ref=e686] [cursor=pointer]:

                  - text: "]. In addition, because NADPH is involved in many enzymatic reactions in intracellular biosynthetic pathways, an unsustainable supply of NADPH not only directly affects the biosynthesis of amino acids but also affects cellular functions and cell survival ["
                  - link "22" [ref=e687] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B22-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. In L-methionine biosynthesis, the unsustainable supply of NADPH is particularly serious, because 8 mol of NADPH is required to synthesize 1 mol of L-methionine, which is much more than that required for the synthesis of other amino acids ["
                  - link "10" [ref=e688] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B10-foods-11-01031"
                  - text: "]. The main source of NADPH in"

                  - text: is the pentose phosphate pathway, and
                  - emphasis [ref=e690]: zwf
                  - text: and
                  - emphasis [ref=e691]: gnd

                  - link "23" [ref=e692] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#B23-foods-11-01031"
