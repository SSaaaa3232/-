## Abstract
![[3ed3fdb9bf04d68810ffcb150ce3ffdd_MD5.svg]]
AI generated
Microbial production of chemicals is an attractive alternative to traditional chemical synthesis, yet challenges in metabolic regulation can limit product yields. This research introduces a dynamic sensor-regulator system (DSRS) designed for enhanced production of fatty acid-based products in Escherichia coli. The DSRS utilizes a transcription factor to sense key intermediates and adjust gene expression dynamically, resulting in improved biodiesel production stability, achieving a titer of 1.5 g/l and a yield increase to 28% of theoretical maximums. The DSRS architecture is versatile and applicable to various biosynthetic pathways, aiming to optimize metabolic flows and boost production efficiency.

[![[ff99b7da46f8233f3483a528d057c887_MD5.jpg]]](https://www.academia.edu/figures/43322836/figure-1-were-placed-of-the-gene-encoding-red-fluorescence)were placed 5’ of the gene encoding red fluorescence protein (rfp) in plasmids pLR-rfp and pAR-rfp, respectively. In the absence of fatty acid, FadR is expected to bind to the 17-bp DNA sequences, which interferes with RNA polymerase binding to the phage promoter and inhibits rfp transcription. When fatty acid is present, the fatty acid is activated to acyl-CoA by acyl-CoA synthase (encoded by fadD). Acyl-CoA in turn binds to FadR and releases FadR from the synthetic promoter, initiating rfp transcription (Fig. la). [![[c42cb4cc343c1b242c92e7154390fd82_MD5.jpg]]](https://www.academia.edu/figures/43322865/figure-1-te-re-sne-noe-rn-cee-ne-be-amon-sme-seed-she-pr-coa)TE Re, SNE NOE, RN cee Ne Be Amon SMe: Seed She Pr CoA biosensors to internally produced fatty acids. To do so, either pLR-rfp or pAR-rfp was transformed into a fatty acid-producing E. coli strain. This strain harbors tesA under the control of P),.yys and produced 3.8 g/l fatty acid after cultivation for 3 d. As compared to wild-type E. coli DH1 harboring pLR-rfp and pAR-rfp, the fatty acid—producing strain harboring the same plasmids was tenfold and 25-fold more fluorescent, respectively (Fig. 1d), and turned the cell culture visibly red (Supplementary Fig. 1). The time [![[0150f98d0f1895b010a75273d2126db9_MD5.jpg]]](https://www.academia.edu/figures/43322894/figure-2-course-of-fluorescence-correlated-well-with-the)course of fluorescence correlated well with the time course of fatty acid production, confirming that the rfp expression was induced by acyl-CoAs activated from the produced fatty acids (Supplementary Fig. 2). Overall, our results indicated that the designed biosensors detected both exogenously added and endogenously produced fatty acids. These sensors can be also used for high-throughput screening of fatty acid—producing strains, and more importantly, to regulate metabolic pathways (Supplementary Fig. 2). [![[8a47a34a50a03b27613b35f68b007eb2_MD5.jpg]]](https://www.academia.edu/figures/43322923/figure-3-had-enhanced-production-titers-and-yields-among)had enhanced production titers and yields (Fig. 3b). Among them, two strains, Y and W, which contain Py; controlling the expression of genes in module B (ethanol pathway) and Pap or Ppy3 controlling the expression of genes in module C (fadD-atfA), had the highest yields. These strains produced threefold higher titers of FAEE compared to that produced by the previously engineered A2A strain, reaching 1.5 g/l after 3 d incubation, corresponding to 28% of the maximum theoretical yield.
