                - generic [ref=e103]:
                  - heading "Background" [level=3] [ref=e104]
                  - paragraph [ref=e105]:
                    - text: Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) is an important cofactor ensuring intracellular redox balance, anabolism and cell growth in all living systems. Our recent multi-omics analyses of glucoamylase (GlaA) biosynthesis in the filamentous fungal cell factory
                    - emphasis [ref=e106]: Aspergillus niger
                    - text: indicated that low availability of NADPH might be a limiting factor for GlaA overproduction.
                - generic [ref=e107]:
                  - heading "Results" [level=3] [ref=e108]
                  - paragraph [ref=e109]:
                    - text: We thus employed the Design-Build-Test-Learn cycle for metabolic engineering to identify and prioritize effective cofactor engineering strategies for GlaA overproduction. Based on available metabolomics and
                    - superscript [ref=e110]: "13"
                    - text: C metabolic flux analysis data, we individually overexpressed seven predicted genes encoding NADPH generation enzymes under the control of the Tet-on gene switch in two
                    - emphasis [ref=e111]: A. niger
                    - text: recipient strains, one carrying a single and one carrying seven
                    - emphasis [ref=e112]: glaA
                    - text: gene copies, respectively, to test their individual effects on GlaA and total protein overproduction. Both strains were selected to understand if a strong pull towards
                    - emphasis [ref=e113]: glaA
                    - text: biosynthesis (seven gene copies) mandates a higher NADPH supply compared to the native condition (one gene copy). Detailed analysis of all 14 strains cultivated in shake flask cultures uncovered that overexpression of the
                    - emphasis [ref=e114]: gsdA
                    - text: gene (glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase),
                    - emphasis [ref=e115]: gndA
                    - text: gene (6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase) and
                    - emphasis [ref=e116]: maeA
                    - text: gene (NADP-dependent malic enzyme) supported GlaA production on a subtle (10%) but significant level in the background strain carrying seven
                    - emphasis [ref=e117]: glaA
                    - text: gene copies. We thus performed maltose-limited chemostat cultures combining metabolome analysis for these three isolates to characterize metabolic-level fluctuations caused by cofactor engineering. In these cultures, overexpression of either the
                    - emphasis [ref=e118]: gndA
                    - text: or
                    - emphasis [ref=e119]: maeA
                    - text: gene increased the intracellular NADPH pool by 45% and 66%, and the yield of GlaA by 65% and 30%, respectively. In contrast, overexpression of the
                    - emphasis [ref=e120]: gsdA
                    - text: gene had a negative effect on both total protein and glucoamylase production.
                - generic [ref=e121]:
                  - heading "Conclusions" [level=3] [ref=e122]
                  - paragraph [ref=e123]:
                    - text: This data suggests for the first time that increased NADPH availability can indeed underpin protein and especially GlaA production in strains where a strong pull towards GlaA biosynthesis exists. This data also indicates that the highest impact on GlaA production can be engineered on a genetic level by increasing the flux through the pentose phosphate pathway (
                    - emphasis [ref=e124]: gndA
                    - text: gene) followed by engineering the flux through the reverse TCA cycle (
                    - emphasis [ref=e125]: maeA
                    - text: gene). We thus propose that NADPH cofactor engineering is indeed a valid strategy for metabolic engineering of
                    - emphasis [ref=e126]: A. niger
                    - text: to improve GlaA production, a strategy which is certainly also applicable to the rational design of other microbial cell factories.
