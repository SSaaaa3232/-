            - paragraph [ref=e120]:
              - text: The year that has just concluded has been a very busy and productive one for the editorial team of Nature Catalysis. We are particularly proud of our effort to maximize our outreach activities and interactions with the scientific community. Collectively, we have attended around 20 international scientific meetings, including a few topical Gordon Research Conferences — for example, the Solar Fuels or the Biocatalysis ones — or for instance the 18th International Congress on Catalysis, which was held in Lyon over July. Participating at conferences represents for us a unique opportunity to closely monitor the most recent developments in the field. Moreover, it is during such meetings that we get the chance to speak to multiple researchers and informally share ideas and points of view with delegates from different nations. Such conversations can be very inspiring and quite often provide the seed for our next editorial project or offer us the possibility to commission a Perspective or a Comment in a more informed manner. It is no surprise that, when possible, we even try to get directly involved in the organization of such a meeting, as in the case of the Nature Conference on

Urea recovery is important both in the context of wastewater purification as well as fertilizer production, and is here achieved through a method that integrates the electrocatalytic synthesis of H

            - paragraph [ref=e144]:
              - text: Iron and the haem co-factor are the protagonists of the biocatalysis articles included in this issue. A first
              - link "Article" [ref=e145] [cursor=pointer]:
                - /url: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-024-01280-8
              - text: deals with a haem-dependent enzyme named piperazate synthase, which catalyses the formation of N–N bonds during the synthesis of piperazate, a common non-proteinogenic amino acid. While such an enzyme has been reported previously, the authors of the present study disclose important structural features that advance the understanding of the catalytic cycle underlying the process of N–N coupling. A second
