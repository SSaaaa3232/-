                  - superscript [ref=e97]: +

                  - emphasis [ref=e99]: Sphingobium
                  - text: sp. SYK-6 was identified for the utilization of synthetic NCBs.

                  - emphasis [ref=e101]: para

                  - text: ) as cofactor. Semi-rational engineering of
                  - emphasis [ref=e104]: Sp

              - generic [ref=e116]:
                - heading "Introduction" [level=2] [ref=e117]: Introduction
                - paragraph [ref=e118]:
                  - text: Alcohol dehydrogenases (ADHs), are crucial industrial biocatalysts that require stoichiometric amounts of cofactors to perform their catalytic functions (Headon and Walsh
                  - link "1994" [ref=e119] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR11"
                  - text: ). However, their application is often limited by the expensive natural cofactors, particularly nicotinamide cofactors, NAD(P)
                  - superscript [ref=e120]: +
                  - text: /NAD(P)H, which comprised 80% of the cofactors used in oxidoreductases (Zachos et al.
                  - link "2019" [ref=e121] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR41"
                  - text: ). Over the years, numerous efforts have been carried out to relieve the cost of cofactors. The most applied approach involves cofactor regeneration, particularly through enzymatic methods (Dai et al.
                  - link "2023" [ref=e122] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR4"
                  - text: ","
                  - link "2024" [ref=e123] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR5"
                  - text: ; Ma et al.
                  - link "2023" [ref=e124] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR25"
                  - text: ; Sun et al.
                  - link "2017" [ref=e125] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR36"
                  - text: ), although these methods introduce unwanted by-products and cosubstrates. Other cofactor regeneration techniques, such as chemical, electrochemical, and photochemical methods suffer from drawbacks such as environmental pollution, low atom utilization, and reliance on photosensitizers or specialized media (Mordhorst and Andexer
                  - link "2020" [ref=e126] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR27"
                  - text: ).
                - paragraph [ref=e127]:
                  - text: To simplify the catalytic process of ADHs and reduce costs, researcher have dedicated to seek low-cost alternatives to natural nicotinamide cofactors. These alternatives, known as nicotinamide cofactor biomimetics (NCBs), have been developed over the past few decades. The first NCBs was synthesized in 1936 to give insights into the chemical properties of cofactors (Karrer and Stare
                  - link "1936" [ref=e128] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR17"
                  - text: ), and were later explored as substitutes for natural cofactors. Semi-synthetic NCBs (ssNCBs), such as nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) and nicotinamide cytosine dinucleotide (NCD), resemble natural cofactors in structure. NMN, for example, is an intermediate in the biosynthesis of NAD (Poddar et al.
                  - link "2019" [ref=e129] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR31"
                  - text: ) and has been applied in glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase as a glucose biosensor (Meng et al.
                  - link "2023" [ref=e130] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR26"
                  - text: ). NCD has been utilized in bio-orthogonal systems, with an intracellular synthetic pathway for NCD synthesis being developed through NCD synthetase (Wang et al.
                  - link "2021" [ref=e131] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR39"
                  - text: ). For totally synthetic NCBs (tsNCBs), they were considered as cost-efficient alternatives for natural cofactors (Guarneri et al.
                  - link "2019" [ref=e132] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR10"
                  - text: ). A thermostable ene-reductase
                  - emphasis [ref=e133]: F
                  - emphasis [ref=e134]: OY
                  - text: E-1 was reported to utilize BNAH as cofactor with 50-fold reduced cost (Tischler et al.
                  - link "2020" [ref=e135] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR38"
                  - text: ). In 2013, simple chemical synthesis routes were introduced, yielding a series of tsNCBs that retain only the nicotinamide moiety of NAD(P)H (Paul et al.
                  - link "2013" [ref=e136] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR30"
                  - text: ). They offered the potential for better catalytic efficiency compared with natural cofactors (Knaus et al.
                  - link "2016" [ref=e137] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR20"
                  - text: ). Further advancements have expanded the range of ssNCBs in the following decade (Falcone et al.
                  - link "2019" [ref=e138] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR6"
                  - text: ; Gu et al.
                  - link "2025" [ref=e139] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR9"
                  - text: ; Li et al.
                  - link "2021" [ref=e140] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR21"
                  - text: ; Nowak et al.
                  - link "2017" [ref=e141] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR29"
                  - text: ).
                - paragraph [ref=e142]:
                  - text: Much of the research in this area has been focused on improving in situ cofactor regeneration (Kara et al.
                  - link "2014" [ref=e143] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR16"
                  - text: ; Zhang and Hollmann
                  - link "2018" [ref=e144] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR42"
                  - text: ), biorthogonal screening (Liu et al.
                  - link "2022" [ref=e145] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR22"
                  - text: ; Richardson et al.
                  - link "2020" [ref=e146] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR33"
                  - text: ) as well as enhancing the activity (Löw et al.
                  - link "2016" [ref=e147] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR24"
                  - text: ; Qi et al.
                  - link "2017" [ref=e148] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR32"
                  - text: ). Unfortunately, the application of tsNCBs in ADH catalysis has been hold up by the scarcity of flavin-free oxidoreductases reported to utilize these cofactors. Instead, the majority findings focused on old yellow enzymes (Nowak et al.
                  - link "2017" [ref=e149] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR29"
                  - text: ; Paul et al.
                  - link "2013" [ref=e150] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR30"
                  - text: ). A NADH oxidase was explored for NCB regeneration, generating 3-carbamoyl-1-methylpyridin-1-ium (MNA
                  - superscript [ref=e151]: +
                  - text: ) and 1-benzyl-3-carbamoylpyridin-1-ium (BNA
                  - superscript [ref=e152]: +
                  - text: ) (Nowak et al.
                  - link "2015" [ref=e153] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR28"
                  - text: ). Some enzyme-NCB combinations outperformed natural combination (Geddes et al.
                  - link "2016" [ref=e154] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR8"
                  - text: ; Knaus et al.
                  - link "2016" [ref=e155] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR20"
                  - text: ). Some even displayed altered substrate spectrum (Löw et al.
                  - link "2016" [ref=e156] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR24"
                  - text: ), broadened the cofactor specificity (Campbell et al.
                  - link "2010" [ref=e157] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR2"
                  - text: ) or shifted redox reaction equilibria (Aspacio et al.
                  - link "2024" [ref=e158] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR1"
                  - text: ). Furthermore, certain ene-reductase showed remarkable asymmetric reduction of C = C bonds and carbon rings, demonstrating excellent conversion and enantioselectivity (Scholtissek et al.
                  - link "2017" [ref=e159] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR35"
                  - text: ; Tan et al.
                  - link "2022" [ref=e160] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR37"
                  - text: ).
                - paragraph [ref=e161]:
                  - text: Despite these advances, the identification of flavin-free oxidoreductases capable of utilizing tsNCBs has remained rare. Glucose dehydrogenase from
                  - emphasis [ref=e162]: Sulfolobus solfataricus
                  - text: was the first flavin-free oxidoreductases to accept tsNCBs (Nowak et al.
                  - link "2017" [ref=e163] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR29"
                  - text: ), and subsequent efforts focused on enhancing its activity and broadening its cofactor spectrum (Huang et al.
                  - link "2019" [ref=e164] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR12"
                  - text: ; Zachos et al.
                  - link "2022" [ref=e165] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR40"
                  - text: ; Zhou et al.
                  - link "2023" [ref=e166] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR43"
                  - text: ) have been reported. The ongoing search for oxidoreductases accepting tsNCBs is a new endeavor, but remains a significant area of research. However, to date, no breakthrough has been made regarding ADH capable of utilizing tsNCBs. Previously, an alcohol dehydrogenase from horse liver (HLADH) was suggested to accept tsNCBs (Jones and Taylor
                  - link "1973" [ref=e167] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR13"
                  - text: ; Lo and Fish
                  - link "2002" [ref=e168] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR23"
                  - text: ), but was later suspected as background activity which could only be detected in crude enzyme (Josa-Culleré et al.
                  - link "2019" [ref=e169] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR14"
                  - text: ), leading to debates about its true capability to utilize NCBs. Consequently, no ADH has been identified that can utilize tsNCBs, and an ADH from
                  - emphasis [ref=e170]: Rhodococcus ruber
                  - text: DSM 44541 has even been used as negative control in studies of NCBs utilization (Rocha et al.
                  - link "2024" [ref=e171] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR34"
                  - text: ).
                - paragraph [ref=e172]:
                  - emphasis [ref=e173]: Sphingobium
                  - text: sp. SYK-6 was first isolated from waste water of a paper mill in Japan, 1987, and was applied in lignin degradation (Katayama et al.
                  - link "1987" [ref=e174] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR18"
                  - text: ). An aldehyde dehydrogenase
                  - emphasis [ref=e175]: Sp
                  - text: ALDH2 from
                  - emphasis [ref=e176]: Sphingobium
                  - text: sp. SYK-6 was found to process broad substrate spectrum (Chen et al.
                  - link "2024" [ref=e177] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR3"
                  - text: ) and was essential in the metabolism of syringaldehyde (Kamimura et al.
                  - link "2017" [ref=e178] [cursor=pointer]:
                    - /url: "#CR15"
                  - text: ). In this study, we identified an ADH from
                  - emphasis [ref=e179]: Sphingobium
                  - text: sp. SYK-6 (
                  - emphasis [ref=e180]: Sp
                  - text: ADH2), representing the first ADH capable of utilizing tsNCBs, particularly toward
                  - emphasis [ref=e181]: para
                  - text: "-3-carbamoyl-1-(4-carboxybenzyl)pyridin-1-ium ("
                  - emphasis [ref=e182]: p
                  - text: "-BANA"
                  - superscript [ref=e183]: +
                  - text: ).
                  - emphasis [ref=e184]: Sp
                  - text: ADH2 displayed distinct substrate spectrum when using
                  - emphasis [ref=e185]: p
                  - text: "-BANA"
                  - superscript [ref=e186]: +
                  - text: as a cofactor. Structural analysis of the enzyme-cofactor complex provides insights into molecular mechanism underlying the improved performance. Variant A290I exhibited a 10-fold increase in specific activity, while the double variant H43L/A290I achieved a remarkable 6750-fold increase in cofactor specificity ratio (CSR, ratio of catalytic efficiency of
                  - emphasis [ref=e187]: p
                  - text: "-BANA"
                  - superscript [ref=e188]: +
                  - text: to NAD
                  - superscript [ref=e189]: +
                  - text: ). Moreover, the cofactor spectrums of the variants were broadened and altered compared with WT. These findings, combined with structural insights gained, provide valuable opportunities for mining and identifying more ADHs favoring mimetic cofactors, and paves way for their potential application in industrial biocatalysis.
